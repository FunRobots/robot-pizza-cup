## The Premise

Most AI hackathons end with a demo on a screen. This one ends with a robot making pizza.

You will receive a low-cost robotic arm and 48 hours to teach it how to manipulate ingredients.

No hard-coded trajectories. No simulated leaderboard. Your model runs on the physical robot.

## How It Works

**01 — Demonstrate.** Teleoperate the SO-101 and show it how to complete the task.

**02 — Collect.** Turn your demonstrations into a real robotics dataset.

**03 — Train.** Fine-tune a robot policy using the LeRobot ecosystem.

**04 — Deploy.** Run your model on the physical robot.

**05 — Compete.** Face standardized evaluation and a hidden generalization challenge.

## Three Challenges

**Precision Topping** — Can your robot reliably pick and precisely place individual toppings?

demonstrations. No retraining. This is the real test.

## FAQ

**Do I need robotics experience?**
No. We provide the robot stack, tutorials and a reference implementation.

**Do I need my own robot?**
No.

**Can I use my own model?**
Yes, provided it satisfies the competition interface and safety requirements.

**Can I use SmolVLA?**
Yes. It is one of the primary intended approaches.

**Can I hard-code the task?**
You may build supporting infrastructure, but the scored autonomous behavior must satisfy the
published competition policy.

**Is it real pizza?**
The benchmark primarily uses standardized reusable props for consistency. The live showcase can use
edible ingredients.

**Why not sauce or dough?**
Those tasks introduce deformable-material and fluid-manipulation complexity that would significantly
increase the failure risk of the inaugural event. Future editions can add them.

**What happens to the data?**
The dataset licensing and contribution policy will be published before participants register.
